01 - Foundations

What are Ethics and Morals?

Two related but distinct concepts that every Home Affairs employee must understand.

Morals

Morals are a person's deeply held values about what is right and wrong. They are shaped by religion, culture, education, lived experiences, and society. They are internal the compass each individual carries from upbringing.

"Wherever there is light, there will always be shadows - morals help us choose which one we stand in."

Ethics

Ethics are formal rules created by an organisation based on shared values. They guide employees on how to behave in the workplace and ensure that everything is done correctly and as intended.

Organisations known for ethical behaviour attract and retain better talent far more successfully than those that do not.

02 - Distinction

The Key Difference

Ethics and morals are related, but they operate at different levels - personal vs. institutional.

Ethics at Home Affairs

Ethics are the formal rules set by the Department of Home Affairs. Violating them carries real consequences disciplinary action, dismissal, and criminal prosecution. They apply to every employee without exception.

Morals as a Person

Morals are personal beliefs shaped by upbringing. A person may feel morally compelled to help a foreign national but this does not override the legal procedures set by the department. Good intentions do not excuse rule-breaking.

Taking bribes is against the DHA's rules - even when it may feel morally justified in the moment.

03 - Corruption

Unethical Behaviour:
Benefits & Consequences

Corruption may offer short-term gain but always leads to serious long-term harm - for the department and the individual.

Impact on The Department

Short-term 'Benefits'

  • Corrupt officials gain immediate extra income through bribes received.
  • Legal procedures are bypassed to process applications quickly in exchange for payment.

These so-called benefits lead only to long-term damage because they are fundamentally illegal.

Consequences

  • Security breaches including the sale of fraudulent IDs and passports.
  • Institutional collapse - corruption creates a shadow system where laws are ignored.
  • The department's core mission is undermined, creating a cycle of dysfunction.
Impact on The Employee

Short-term 'Benefits'

  • Quick illegal income - officials skip legal procedures in exchange for bribes.
  • Higher standards of living - employees can afford goods beyond their legitimate pay grade.

Money is the cause of all this - not morals, not principle. Just money.

Consequences

  • Immediate dismissal following a disciplinary hearing.
  • Cases are referred to law enforcement for criminal prosecution.
  • Convicted officials face prison sentences ranging from 4 to 18 years.
  • A criminal record prevents future employment in both public and private sectors.
⚠   Corruption does not just harm the corrupt - it harms every South African who depends on the Department of Home Affairs.

04 - Root Causes

Why Does This Happen?

Understanding the root causes of corruption is the first step toward preventing it.

01

Lack of Consequences

When officials face no meaningful repercussions, unethical behaviour continues unchecked and becomes normalised within the system.

02

Low Wages

Employees receiving low wages are vulnerable. Financial pressure can push individuals toward corruption as a means of survival.

03

Poor Management

Weak oversight creates environments where misconduct can go undetected. Good supervision is a powerful deterrent.

04

Lack of Ethics Training

Without proper training, officials may not fully understand the rules, consequences, or the importance of ethical conduct.

05

Greed

Some officials engage in corruption purely out of greed - not necessity. Personal ambition for wealth overrides duty to the public.

05 - Importance

Why Ethics Matter

Ethical conduct protects the integrity of the Department of Home Affairs, strengthens public confidence, and ensures that every citizen is treated fairly and lawfully.

Public Trust

Ensures citizens can rely on the integrity of government documents and public services.

National Security

Protects the borders, identity systems, and national interests of the Republic.

Professionalism

Encourages a workplace built on accountability, respect, and lawful conduct.

Legal Safety

Helps prevent criminal liability, disciplinary action, and institutional misconduct.
